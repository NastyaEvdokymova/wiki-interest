# Report template

Structure of the one-page PDF (`wikitrends report`). Use the same order
when the user asks for a short answer in chat rather than a file.

---

**Topic interest: {label}**
{qid} · period {period} · metric {metric} · report date {date}

## Summary

One line per language, taken verbatim from the `verdicts` field:

- {lang}: interest is {growing|declining} by {X}% per year (~{share} views per million); confidence {high|medium|low}
- {lang}: interest is stable (~{share} views per million), no significant trend; confidence {high|medium|low}

## Chart

Normalised monthly series smoothed with a rolling median.
Y axis: views per million views of the language edition.

## By language

| Lang | Article | Views/mo | Per M | Trend, %/yr | Signif. | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| {lang} | {title} | {monthly_views} | {share_per_million} | {change_pct_per_year} | {yes/no} | {level} |

## Recommendation

Generated by code from the ranking. The criteria are printed next to the conclusion:
`volume=0.5, growth=0.5` by default, otherwise the values from `--weights`, plus the
`--min-confidence` / `--min-views` thresholds.

- "Why this order": one line per ranked language from `recommendation.why`.
- "Research next": up to three languages from `recommendation.research_next`, never `low` confidence.
- "Excluded from the ranking": languages from `excluded` with the reason.

## Assumptions and limitations

First the `confidence.reasons` for each language, then the general
limitations from `LIMITATIONS`. Do not shorten or soften any item.

---

## What must not be in the report

- Estimates of market size, revenue or number of customers.
- Confidence higher than the tool reported.
- Comparison of absolute views across languages.
- Conclusions for languages without an article: list them as "no data".
